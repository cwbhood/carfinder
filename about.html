How these are built

Open by default
Code, data, tax research notes, tests and design notes live in the repository. Anyone can read how a number was computed, and why.
Vibe coded with Claude
Claude does the research and the heavy lifting on code and content. A person sets the goals, reviews the work and owns the decisions, including what ships and when.
Small, testable steps
Every dataset row is validated, the pricing core runs for every vehicle in every region, known real-world quotes are asserted, and the page is driven headlessly at phone and desktop widths before it ships.
Honest about the numbers
Dealer prices are bands, taxes follow the published rules on the compile date, exchange rates are dated, and each tab ends with a plain note on what was assumed. How we calculate has every table and its source.

Credit and contact

CarFinder is free to use, read, fork and build on under the MIT licence; all that is asked is that the copyright line stays with the code. Every release is archived on Zenodo. If you write about it, teach with it or build on it, cite it like this:

Jones, D. (2026). CarFinder 2026 [Data set and software]. Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23382016

The DOI always resolves to the latest version. The repository's CITATION.cff gives the same reference in BibTeX and APA through GitHub's “Cite this repository” button, and the dataset page lists every file. Vehicle prices and specifications belong to their manufacturers and are compiled here for reference; the fonts are under the SIL Open Font License.
