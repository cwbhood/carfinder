“What's your monthly budget?”

The oldest question on the lot, and the reason the drive-off number matters.

A monthly payment is a price with the loan term hidden inside it. Keep the payment the same, stretch the loan, and a more expensive car fits the same budget. The dealer chooses the term, and you negotiate against yourself.

The table uses the loan defaults on CarFinder's US tab: 7% APR and 10% down. The $399 is an example figure; the arithmetic is real, and every assumption is editable in the app.

How to use it

Nothing to install and nothing to sign up for. The page remembers your choices in your own browser.

  1. Pick cars, motorcycles, boats or aircraft, then your country

    The switch at the top changes the kind of vehicle; the country menu changes the vehicles, the price basis (MSRP, ex-showroom, OTR, RRP…), the tax rules and the glossary. World pools every country in one list.

  2. Pick your state or city

    Every drive-off price recomputes for its taxes and fees. In California and New York, pick the city or county too.

  3. Narrow it down

    Tap the body and fuel chips, or open Filters for price, size, seats, drivetrain, minimum MPG and electric range. Or press Help me choose and answer four questions.

  4. Sort by what matters to you

    Price, yearly fuel cost, MPG, electric range, horsepower, price per horsepower, seats.

  5. Open a card, then compare

    The sheet has the photo, the itemised breakdown and a trim slider from base to top trim. Add up to four cards to compare side by side.

The fine print

Many car sites want your phone number so a dealer can call. This one has no form to fill in, and it shows its working.

Every price shows its source
Each detail sheet links the maker's build-and-price page, says when the list was compiled, which of four states the row is in (checked, listed, converted or estimated) and why, and which region's rules were applied, with the state page behind them.
How we calculate
The methodology publishes every tax table with a source column, the dealer bands and the dated exchange rates, generated from the same data the app ships with.
A measured scorecard
The data-quality scorecard counts how many rows are checked against a published price list and how many are estimates, release by release (in the repository).
Corrections in the open
Every sheet has Wrong price? Tell us, a prefilled issue, and Copy the details for an email. Fixes are listed under corrections and in the changelog.
The dataset, citable
44 JSON files of price rows plus the tax and fee rules, MIT licensed, each release archived on Zenodo: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23382016.
Tested before it ships
node --test checks every row, runs the pricing engines for all 97,000 vehicle-and-region combinations and asserts known quotes. A headless Chromium pass drives every control at phone and desktop widths.
Estimates, labelled
Dealer prices are bands by demand, not quotes. Modelled prices carry an Estimate tag and converted lists a Converted tag, and every tab ends with a plain note on what was assumed.
No account, no ads, no trackers
No sign-up, no cookies, nobody paying to put a car in front of you. Your choices stay in your own browser.
One file, open source
Markup, CSS, plain JavaScript and the data in one HTML page that keeps working offline once loaded. MIT licence on GitHub; a wrong rate is a one-line fix and a pull request.
git clone https://github.com/destinjones/carfinder
cd carfinder
node src/build.js        # stitches src/ + data/ into carfinder.html
node --test              # schema checks, pricing core, known quotes
python3 -m http.server   # then open http://localhost:8000/

Built by one person and an AI, in the open

I had the question every first-time buyer has, “what will this actually cost me?”, and instead of googling it fifty times I described the tool I wanted and vibe coded it with Claude. I set the direction and made the calls; Claude did the research and the heavy lifting on the code. All of it ships in the open, so you can check every number.

Read it, fork it, fix a price. Same way I built Open Overwatch, a live map of the whole planet, and Ironbound, an open source RTS.

A starting point, not a quote

Car prices were compiled on 29 September 2026, motorcycles and boats on 10 October and aircraft on 12 October, from manufacturer price lists and the main listing sites in each country. Most bike and boat rows outside the US are estimates until a published price list is found, and aircraft outside the US are converted from the maker's dollar or euro list at a dated rate. Incentives, fuel prices and exchange rates move monthly; tax rules change with budgets.

Get the out-the-door price in writing from two or three dealers. If you spot a wrong price or rate, report it or fix the JSON and send a pull request.

The dealer hopes you never open this.
