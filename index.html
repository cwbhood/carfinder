Why this exists

“What's your monthly budget?” is the oldest trick on the lot.

It works because you don't know the drive-off number. You can't hold a $42,000 sticker and a $399 payment in your head at the same time, so you negotiate against yourself. CarFinder breaks the trick by putting all three prices on every card.

  • ListThe maker's price, from the base trim to the top one, with the mandatory destination charge underneath.
  • DealerThe band dealers really sell that model in this month: at sticker, a little under, or deep discounts.
  • Drive-offThe estimated total once your state's tax, title, doc fee and registration are added. The number that leaves your account.

Here is the trick in numbers. A monthly payment is a price with the term hidden inside it. Keep the payment the same, stretch the loan, and a more expensive car fits the same “budget.” The dealer chooses the term.

The table uses the loan defaults on CarFinder's US tab: 7% APR and 10% down. The $399 and $42,000 above are example figures; the math is real, and you can change every assumption on the page.

Try it on your own state

Pick your state. Watch every price move.

Choose a US state, an Indian state, a Chinese city, an Australian state, a Mexican estado, a Spanish region, a Russian region, a Brazilian state or a Canadian province, and every drive-off price on the page recomputes with its sales tax, road tax, plates, registration, insurance and rebates. Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for.

  1. Pick cars, motorcycles or boats, then your country

    The three buttons at the very top switch the kind of vehicle; the country tabs under them switch the whole page: the vehicles, the price basis (MSRP, ex-showroom, OTR, 指导价, RRP, ride-away…), the tax rules and the glossary. World pools every country in one list.

  2. Pick your state or city

    The picker under the search bar (next to it on a desktop) sets the taxes and fees. Every drive-off price recomputes for it, and the page remembers your choice in your own browser.

  3. Narrow it down

    Tap the body-type and fuel chips, or open Filters for price range, size, brand tier, seats, drivetrain, minimum MPG and minimum electric range. For bikes the filters become engine size and transmission; for boats, length, capacity, propulsion and fuel burn. Or press Help me choose and answer four questions.

  4. Sort by what matters to you

    Price, lowest yearly fuel cost, best MPG, longest electric range, most horsepower, cheapest per horsepower, most seats. On a phone the sort lives behind the ⇅ button next to the search box.

  5. Open a card, then compare

    Tap any card for its photo, the cost breakdown and the trim slider from base to top trim. Tap + Compare on up to four cards and see them side by side.

What it feels like

Sit down already holding the dealer's playbook.

Every card answers the questions the salesperson would rather you didn't ask: is this model selling at sticker or sitting on the lot, which fees are real, and which ones you can push back on.

01Is this car hot or sitting?
The market badge says it plainly. Red: sells at or above sticker, expect a wait. Grey-blue: small discounts are typical. Green: big discounts and incentives; this is where the deals are.
02What should I actually pay?
The drive-off range starts from the sticker, applies the typical dealer discount or markup for that model, then adds your state's tax, a typical doc fee and registration. Bargain within it; don't accept above it.
03Which lines can I negotiate?
Open a car and drag the trim slider from base to fully loaded. The breakdown updates line by line: destination, discount, tax, doc fee, registration, insurance, grants, each with a note on what it is and whether you can negotiate it.
04What do I say to the dealer?
The Why this price band panel explains the market for that model and what to ask the dealer.
05What does it cost after I drive off?
Yearly fuel or charging cost at your local prices and distance, an estimated monthly payment, and the assumptions behind them, all editable. You set the term, not the dealer.

The trust stack

Many car sites want your phone number. This one wants nothing.

Fill in a form on many car sites and your details go to dealers who will call you about it. CarFinder has no form to fill in.

No account

No sign-up, no email, no phone number. Open the page and use it.

No ads, no cookies, no trackers

Nobody is paying to put a car in front of you. Your state choice is saved in your own browser and goes nowhere else.

One file

Markup, CSS, plain JavaScript and the data in one self-contained HTML page. It keeps working offline once loaded.

Open data

Each country's cars, bikes and boats are JSON files with a documented row schema; each country's car taxes are one function and its bike and boat fees one table. MIT licence. A wrong rate is a one-line fix and a pull request.

Tested before it ships

node --test checks every row of every dataset, then runs the pricing engines for all 83,000 vehicle-and-region combinations and checks known quotes. A headless Chromium pass drives every tab and control of all three verticals at phone and desktop widths.

Estimates, labelled as estimates

Dealer prices are bands by demand level, not quotes. Taxes follow the published rules on the compile date. Every tab ends with a plain note on what was assumed, and provisional prices carry a Preliminary tag.

git clone https://github.com/destinjones/carfinder
cd carfinder
node src/build.js        # stitches src/ + data/ into carfinder.html
node --test              # schema checks, pricing core, known quotes
python3 -m http.server   # then open http://localhost:8000/

Before you go

Use it as a starting point, not a quote.

Car prices were compiled on 29 September 2026 and motorcycle and boat prices on 10 October 2026, from manufacturer price lists and the main listing sites in each country; most bike and boat rows outside the US are marked preliminary until a dealer price list confirms them. Incentives, fuel prices and exchange rates move monthly, and tax rules change with budgets. Always get the out-the-door price in writing from two or three dealers. If you spot a wrong price or rate, open an issue or fix the JSON and send a pull request.

The dealer hopes you never open this. Open it.
